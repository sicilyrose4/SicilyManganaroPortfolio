Bull Stock - Learn. Invest. Grow.

A beginner investing app for recent graduates. Built from interviews with new investors, it lets people learn the stock market and practice with mock money before risking their own.

ROLE
UX/UI design, from research and wireframes to the final screens, in collaboration with my team. I owned the branding.
TEAM
Team of three
TIMELINE
4 weeks, Fall 2025

Recent graduates want to invest, but the stock market feels like gambling with money they barely have.

Research

Our brief was open: pick an audience in fintech, find a real struggle, and design for it. We chose recent graduates entering the stock market for the first time, and set four goals for our research.

  1. 01Understand what users need when entering the stock market
  2. 02Understand how they currently learn about the market and start investing
  3. 03Understand the pain points of recent grads who invest
  4. 04Understand their perceptions and goals around the stock market

Secondary research

Studies on Gen Z investors: where they learn, what holds them back, what they want from a platform.

Competitor review

Eight investing apps, from Robinhood and Acorns to Fidelity and Webull, compared for how they treat beginners.

Interviews

Conversations with recent grads about how they see the market, their goals, and what scares them.

Current thoughts on investing?

Recent graduates feel overwhelmed and intimidated by the stock market. Investing feels a little like gambling, and on a limited budget they don’t want to risk money on something they don’t understand.

  • “I want to invest, but I’m scared of losing money I barely have.”
  • “I want to invest, not gamble. But I can’t tell the difference.”
  • “I feel anxious about investing because it is too risky.”
  • “Having a community (with the stock market) is crucial to me.”
  • “I don’t understand any of the trends.”

Who we designed for

Leo is our persona: a recent graduate with a first job, student debt, and no confidence with investing terms.

User journey

We mapped the path from first hearing about investing to managing a portfolio. The sharpest pain came right after downloading an app.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

How might we reduce fear and anxiety, and build confidence for beginners to safely experiment with stock market investing?

What testing changed

We put the prototype in front of users and fixed what tripped them up.

Confidence scale

On the questionnaire, the 1–5 confidence scale had no labels, so the numbers were open to interpretation. We added a label under the scale.

Choosing a mode

Tapping Mock or Real Mode used to enter the app straight away, which left room for error. We added an Enter button, then made the two choices look clearly different and explained what each one means.

Knowing which mode you’re in

Color alone (pink for Mock, blue for Real) wasn’t enough. A “Mock Mode” or “Real Mode” label now sits at the top left of the screen.

Finding tutorials

People weren’t sure what the boxes at the top of Home were, or that there were more. We added a “Tutorials” label and moved “See all tutorials” to the top of the row.

Search, not add

The center button searches for stocks and adds them to a portfolio. Testing showed “search” is the first thing people do there, so the plus became a search icon.

Explaining the jargon

Terms like market capitalization and dividend yield confused new investors. Each box got a question mark that explains the term in place.

Tutorials in Community

Tutorials were buried in the profile. They now live in the Community tab, next to News.

Showing progress

Tutorials gave no sense of how far along you were. We added a progress bar and a “percent completed” label.

Reflection

What we set out to do

Our goal was to ease the anxiety recent grads feel about entering the stock market. We built an app that puts learning within easy reach and makes a confusing topic feel calmer.

What I’d do next

Looking back a year later, I’d rework the visual design. The contrast is a bit high, and I might explore a different style if I were the only one working on it. I feel confident in the UX, though, because every decision traces back to research. In the end, though, there are always improvements to be made with more user research.
